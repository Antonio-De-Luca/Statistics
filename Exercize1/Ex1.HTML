Interpretation and cryptographic relevance

Coordinate distribution: The x and y coordinates are field elements modulo p. Their values are constrained by the curve equation; they are not independent random numbers.

Value frequencies: Repeated coordinates can be counted in a sample. A lack of repetitions in a small sample does not establish uniformity.

Bit distribution: Counting zeroes and ones is a useful descriptive statistic. Balanced bit counts alone do not demonstrate unpredictability.

Apparent uniformity: A cryptographic public-key mapping should not reveal an obvious, exploitable pattern in its outputs. However, uniform-looking samples are insufficient to establish security.

Input-output correlation: Simple correlations can reveal some patterns, but a low linear correlation does not prove that recovering the private scalar is computationally infeasible.

Avalanche-like behaviour: A large change in output bits for a small input change is a familiar hash-function design goal. Scalar multiplication is not required to satisfy the strict hash avalanche criterion.
