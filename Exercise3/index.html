Stochastic Penetration Models

Law of Large Numbers, variance scaling, and the Central Limit Theorem for Bernoulli and Rademacher random walks.

Model penetration scores across n steps and simulate m independent trajectories. Explore how the total, average, and normalized total behave as the system size increases.

Simulation parameters

Each attacker generates an independent trajectory. The same n and m are used for both models. A fresh simulation is generated on each run.

MODEL A

Bernoulli model

Independent increments Xᵢ ∈ {0,1}, with P(Xᵢ = 1) = p. A successful step contributes one unit; an unsuccessful step contributes zero.

Theoretical properties

Mean of an increment: E[Xᵢ] = p

Variance of an increment: Var(Xᵢ) = p(1 − p)

Total: Sₙ ~ Binomial(n,p)

Average converges in probability to p, not necessarily zero.

MODEL B

Rademacher model

Independent increments Xᵢ ∈ {−1,+1}, with equal probability 1/2. Positive and negative contributions are equally likely.

Theoretical properties

Mean of an increment: E[Xᵢ] = 0

Variance of an increment: Var(Xᵢ) = 1

The average converges in probability to zero; the normalized total converges in distribution to a standard normal.

Mathematical interpretation

1. Total score

Sₙ = X₁ + X₂ + ··· + Xₙ

By independence, means and variances add. In the Bernoulli model, E[Sₙ] = np and Var(Sₙ) = np(1 − p). In the Rademacher model, E[Sₙ] = 0 and Var(Sₙ) = n.

Thus, total scores typically grow in magnitude with system size: the Bernoulli total grows linearly when p is positive, while the centered Rademacher total fluctuates on the order of √n.

2. Average score and the Law of Large Numbers

Sₙ/n → E[X₁] in probability

The Bernoulli average approaches p, and the Rademacher average approaches zero. Their variances are p(1 − p)/n and 1/n, respectively. These variances tend to zero, so the averages concentrate around their expected values.

3. Normalized total and variance

Sₙ/√n

The normalized total has variance p(1 − p) in the Bernoulli model and 1 in the Rademacher model, regardless of n. The normalization prevents variance from growing with n.

However, the Bernoulli normalized total has mean p√n. Unless p = 0, it does not converge to a standard normal distribution. To apply the Central Limit Theorem to Bernoulli increments, center and scale the sum:

(Sₙ − np) / √(np(1 − p)) → N(0,1), for 0 < p < 1

4. Central Limit Theorem for Rademacher increments

Sₙ/√n → N(0,1) in distribution

Each Rademacher increment has mean zero and variance one. As n increases, the discrete distribution of the normalized sum approaches the bell-shaped standard normal distribution. Individual trajectories remain random; it is the distribution across repeated trajectories that converges.

Interpretation of attackers and systems: n controls the number of steps contributing to each attacker's score. m controls how many independent outcomes are observed. Increasing n reveals the limiting behavior of the stochastic process; increasing m improves the empirical estimate of its distribution. This model assumes independent increments and independent attackers—it does not explicitly model interactions between attackers or dependencies between systems.

Effect of System Size and Number of Attackers

  • System size (n): The number of steps in the penetration process. As n increases, the aggregate penetration score becomes more stable relative to its expected value. Under the Bernoulli model, the expected total score grows as np, while its variance grows as np(1-p). Under the Rademacher model, the expected total is 0 and the variance grows as n.
  • Number of attackers (m): Increasing m produces more independent simulated penetration trajectories. This does not change the theoretical distribution of an individual attacker's score, but it improves the accuracy of empirical estimates, such as the sample mean and variance.
  • Aggregate penetration score: If the aggregate score is defined as the sum across all m attackers and all n steps, its expected value and variance are multiplied by m, assuming all increments are independent. Specifically, the Bernoulli model has expected aggregate score mnp and variance mnp(1-p), while the Rademacher model has expected aggregate score 0 and variance mn.
  • Interpretation: Increasing n increases the amount of penetration activity represented in the score, whereas increasing m improves the statistical reliability of the simulation. These effects should be distinguished: a larger sample of attackers does not necessarily mean that each attacker is more effective.
