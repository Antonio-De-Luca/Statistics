Contingency Tables and Chi-Square Test

Analysis of bivariate categorical data, marginal and conditional frequencies, statistical independence, and the Chi-square test.

Contingency Tables

A bivariate dataset contains observations of two variables, X and F. When both variables are categorical, their joint distribution can be represented by a contingency table.

Each cell contains the observed frequency Oij, which counts the observations belonging to category i of X and category j of F.

Total Sample Size
\[ n=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{c}O_{ij} \]

Marginal and Conditional Frequencies

Marginal frequencies describe the distribution of each variable independently of the other.

Marginal Absolute Frequencies
\[ n_{i\cdot}=\sum_{j=1}^{c}O_{ij}, \qquad n_{\cdot j}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}O_{ij} \]
Marginal Relative Frequencies
\[ f_{i\cdot}=\frac{n_{i\cdot}}{n}, \qquad f_{\cdot j}=\frac{n_{\cdot j}}{n} \]

Conditional frequencies describe the distribution of one variable when the category of the other variable is known.

Conditional Relative Frequencies
\[ f_{j|i}=\frac{O_{ij}}{n_{i\cdot}}, \qquad f_{i|j}=\frac{O_{ij}}{n_{\cdot j}} \]

Statistical Independence

Two random variables X and F are independent if knowing the value of one does not change the probability distribution of the other.

Definition of Independence
\[ P(X=x_i,F=f_j) = P(X=x_i)P(F=f_j) \]

Equivalently, when the conditioning event has positive probability:

\[ P(F=f_j\mid X=x_i)=P(F=f_j) \]

If the variables are independent, the joint probability distribution is completely determined by their marginal distributions.

Expected Frequencies under Independence

If the two variables are independent, the expected frequency of a cell is calculated using the corresponding row and column totals.

Expected Frequency
\[ E_{ij}= \frac{n_{i\cdot}n_{\cdot j}}{n} \]

These values describe the frequencies that would be expected if the independence hypothesis were true.

Chi-Square Test of Independence

The Chi-square test compares observed frequencies with expected frequencies under the assumption of independence.

Chi-Square Statistic
\[ \chi^2= \sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{c} \frac{(O_{ij}-E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}} \]
Degrees of Freedom
\[ df=(r-1)(c-1) \]

The hypotheses are:

  • H₀: X and F are statistically independent.
  • H₁: X and F are statistically dependent.

We use a significance level of α = 0.05. If the p-value is smaller than α, we reject the null hypothesis.
